Printables

{printable:labels} makes pages for paper - sheets of labels, name badges, business cards, tickets with a stub, a certificate, envelopes, a letter and an invoice for a window envelope, index cards and place cards. There are 14 kinds - every kind is a file of pad/subs/printables/types/, and the printables application shows them all, each with its PDF.

PHP
 HTML
fragments/printables_1.pad 		Result 
{fake:rows 'name, street, postcode, city', rows=5, seed=21, toData='people'/}
{printable:labels data='people', sheet='a4-2x7', scale=0.4}
  <b>{$name}</b><br>{$street}<br>{$postcode} {$city}
{/printable:labels}

One item, repeated over the data

The content between the tags is one item - a label, a badge, a letter - rendered once for every row of the data, with the handling options of any tag: sort=, where=, first=. copies= writes every item that many times, skip= leaves the first labels of a sheet used before empty. A kind takes the parts of an item from the elements that say what they are: a ticket's stub is its <aside>, a letter's address its <address>.

PHP
 HTML
fragments/printables_2.pad 		Result 
{pad:data 'seats'}
[ { "row": "A", "seat": 4 }, { "row": "B", "seat": 7 } ]
{/pad:data}
{printable:tickets data='seats', scale=0.4}
  <h2>Hamlet</h2>Row {$row}, seat {$seat}
  <aside>{barcode:qr "seat-{$row}{$seat}", size=80/}</aside>
{/printable:tickets}
2 tickets on 1 sheet of A4, 210 × 297 mm

The screen, the paper and the PDF

On the screen the sheets lie on a grey desk at their size in millimetres - scale= makes the preview smaller. Printed, the page prints the sheets alone, each on a page of its own size without a margin. And in a {pad:pdf}, or with pdf on the tag, PAD's own PDF writer lays out the same sheets: pdf links them, the page asked again with &padPdf.

PHP
 HTML
fragments/printables_3.pad 		Result 
{printable:envelope sender='PAD, Main Street 1, 3511 AB Utrecht', scale=0.5, pdf}
  Ann Smith<br>Canal Street 12<br>1012 AB Amsterdam
{/printable:envelope}
1 envelope on 1 sheet of DL, 220 × 110 mm

Stamp
  • A sheet of one's own: across=, down=, width= and height= of a label, margin= and pitch=, all in millimetres.
  • Every item is one region of its sheet: what does not fit is cut at its edge - a letter is one page.
  • The PDF has no CSS of the page: it sets the text in Helvetica, Times or Courier, a table in its own grid, and draws the barcodes and QR codes but no other picture than a JPEG.
