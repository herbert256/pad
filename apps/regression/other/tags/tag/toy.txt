<p><code>{toy}</code> puts a small live simulation or visual toy on the page, run in the browser: the
server checks the options it is given and writes its frame, and the toy's script - written once per
page, with the CSP nonce - animates it. Every toy is a file of <code>pad/toys/types/</code>, its
script and its style in <code>pad/toys/assets/</code>; the files of <code>types/</code> are the toys
there are.</p>

<p>A toy is written three ways, the same toy each time: the tag with the toy's name,
<code>{toy 'life'/}</code>; the toy: type, <code>{toy:life/}</code>; and the bare name,
<code>{life/}</code>, when no tag, store, field or sequence has that name. The pattern of a Game of
Life and the rules of an L-system stand between the tags, one a line - taken as written, before the
level walks them, so a field or a tag there is not filled in.</p>

<p>Every toy can be touched - draw cells, drag a bob, throw a planet, stir the current - and has
labelled controls and a status line; its canvas has <code>role="img"</code> and a label that says what
it shows. A toy holds while the page is hidden or it is scrolled out of view, and waits for its Play
button when the visitor asks for reduced motion or the tag says <code>paused</code>. Its root carries
<code>data-state</code>: <code>playing</code>, <code>paused</code> or <code>held</code>.</p>

<p><code>seed=</code> makes what a toy picks at random the same on every load. The colours are
<code>--pad-toy-ink</code>, <code>-canvas</code>, <code>-accent</code>, <code>-night</code> and their
like on <code>.pad-toy</code>, light and dark; the script resolves them for the canvas and again when
the scheme changes.</p>
