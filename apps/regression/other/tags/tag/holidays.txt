<p><code>{holidays 'nl', year=2026}...{/holidays}</code> hands over the public holidays of the
Netherlands in 2026 as rows, sorted by date: <code>$date</code>, <code>$name</code> in English,
<code>$local</code> in the country's own language, <code>$type</code> - <code>public</code> for
a day off, <code>observance</code> for a national day that is none - <code>$region</code> for a
regional holiday and <code>$substitute</code> for the day that makes up for a holiday on a
weekend. As a single tag it writes them as a table.</p>

<p>No service is asked and no table of dates kept: every country is a file of rules in
<code>pad/subs/holidays/types/</code> - a fixed date, days from Easter Sunday by the Gregorian
computus, the nth or last weekday of a month, the years a holiday is held or moved, the move
off a Sunday of King's Day, the substitute days of the United Kingdom (the next free weekday)
and the United States (the Friday before, the Monday after). A country is added by adding its
file.</p>

<p>A region follows its country, <code>de-by</code>, or stands in <code>region=</code>. The
handling options work on the rows - <code>where='$type eq "public"'</code> leaves the
observances out. <code>holidays=</code> on <code>{calendar}</code> marks them in a month, and
<code>padHolidays</code>, <code>padIsHoliday</code>, <code>padWorkdays</code> and
<code>padAddWorkdays</code> answer a page's PHP. A country without a file, a region it has
not, a year out of range or a <code>lang</code> it has no names in is a strict error.</p>
