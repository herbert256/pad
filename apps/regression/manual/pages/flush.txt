<p>A page is sent in one piece, once all of it is rendered. {flush} sends what is
rendered so far straight away: put after the wrapper's <code>&lt;/head&gt;</code>, it lets
the browser fetch the stylesheets and fonts while a slow part of the page is still being
rendered.</p>

<p>Shown here inside another page, the fragment flushes nothing - only the page that is the
response does. Asked for on its own, it sends its first line before the second is rendered;
either way the page arrives whole.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Everything that needs the whole page is off for a request that flushes: tidy, gzip of
      the whole body, the ETag and its 304, <code>Content-Length</code>, the page cache. The
      headers go out with the first part.</li>
  <li>It stands at the top level of the page or its wrapper - not inside another tag. The
      page's PHP runs before any of the template, so what a flush wins is the rendering
      below it: a big table, a {curl}, a select.</li>
</ul>
