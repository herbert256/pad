{spaceless} renders its content first and then removes every run of whitespace that stands between a > and a <, newlines included, and the whitespace at both ends. <li>a</li> <li>b</li> becomes <li>a</li><li>b</li>; the text inside an element keeps its spaces.

That matters where whitespace shows: between inline-block elements a newline in the template is a visible gap, and in a <pre> or a JSON-like attribute every character counts. The template can stay indented for the reader while the output is compact.

For one tag at a time the ~ whitespace control does the same job without a pair: {items} takes the whitespace on both sides of the tag, newlines included. {tidy} goes the other way and indents the markup.

The tag only makes sense as a pair: written alone, the strict check reports that it never closes.
