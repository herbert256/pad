{pad:continue} abandons what is left of the current pass of a loop - everything of the loop's content after the tag is dropped - and the loop carries on with its next row, like PHP's continue. It is usually written inside an {pad:if}, to skip the rows that do not qualify. The rows are untouched; that is the difference with {pad:break}, which empties them.

Without a parameter it works on the nearest enclosing loop: the data tag, {sequence:*}, {pad:while} or other loop the tag stands in, past an {pad:if}, a {pad:case} and any other level that renders once for the row - an {pad:ifchanged}, a {pad:guest}, a snippet. With a name - {pad:continue 'orders'/}, the tag's name or its name= - it reaches an outer loop: the inner loop is abandoned with the rest of the outer pass. A level number or a negative offset (-2: two levels up) picks the level by position.

Under the strict syntax check a name that no enclosing level has, or an offset past the page, is an error rather than a silent fall-back to the nearest loop; so is loop control with no loop around it. To skip rows that can be told apart by their fields alone, the where option on the loop is simpler.
