The Select subsystem makes the tables of a database tags. Declare a table once - its key, its order - and the relations between the tables, and a template walks them without writing a join: a table nested in another reads only the rows related to the row around it. Every example here shows the declarations it uses, its template, the result and the SQL PAD sent for it, over a small music catalogue - the model.

Tables

A declared table is a tag

  • A table is a tagDeclared in $padSelect, a table is a tag of its own name: the pair walks its rows, sorted by the declared order, and every column is a field.
  • The select: prefixselect: names the type, for a table whose name is something else as well - here a data store of the page called labels too.
  • A row by its key$field=value on the tag binds a field of the declared key - one field, or both of a key of two. The value goes into the SQL escaped, never as SQL.
  • where= with bound valueswhere= is SQL the template writes, as a quoted string; a value goes into it as $name and is bound as an escaped literal of that variable.
  • order= and rows=order= sorts in the SQL and rows= is its limit, so the longest five tracks are the five longest of them all, not five sorted afterwards.
  • fields= : the columnsfields= on the tag is the column list as SQL, with expressions and aliases; declared as an array it is column => alias.
  • db= : a tag of its own nameThe tag name is yours, db= names the table it reads - two tags over one table, each with its own order and where.
  • No rows: @else@A select that finds nothing renders its @else@ part, as every tag with no rows does.

Relations

Nested tables follow their relations

  • Parent to childrenNested in {select:artists}, {select:albums} reads only the albums of the artist of the row: the relation albums - artists says which field they share.
  • Child to parentThe same relation the other way round: nested in an album, {select:artists} is the one artist the album belongs to.
  • Three levels deepArtists, their albums, the tracks of each album - every level follows the relation to the level around it.
  • A relation by the other key[ 'key' => 'signed_to' ]: the field of artists that holds the declared key of labels. An artist with no label relates to no label.
  • A relation over two fieldsA playlist entry points at a track by album and track number: the relation maps both fields, and the chain goes on to the album and its artist.
  • A table related to itselfTwo views over genres, one for the parent and one for the sub-genres, related to genres by parent_code.
  • Every enclosing select countsAlbums inside an artist inside a genre: the albums follow both relations, so each artist shows only the albums of that genre.
  • Counting related rowsfields='count(*) as n' on a nested table counts the related rows in the SQL.

Views

Tables that stand on another

  • A view over a tablebase= makes a virtual table: the base table with a where and an order of its own, used as a tag like any table.
  • A view of a viewA view can stand on another view: the chain is followed to the table at its end, and the where of every view holds.
  • A view follows its base relationsNested in {select:artists}, the view of recent albums is related by the relation of albums, the table it stands on.
  • where= on a view narrows itA where= on the tag of a view holds beside the view's own: the recent albums that are under 14, never an album the view leaves out.

Joins

More than one table in a row

  • Inner joinjoin= in the declaration: the joined table, its key met by key=, and the columns it adds - its declared fields, column => alias.
  • Left joinA left join keeps the row that has no partner: the artist without a label is listed, its label NULL.
  • Natural joinA natural join meets the columns both tables have - genre_code - with no key to give.
  • More than one joinA list of joins: a track with the title of its album and the name of the artist, in one statement.
  • A join to a viewA joined table with a where of its own joins only its rows: it stands in the statement as a derived table under its table's name.
  • join= on the tagOn the tag join= is the SQL after the word join, written as a quoted string.

Aggregates

Groups, totals and distinct rows

  • group= : a row per groupgroup= with aggregate fields: the albums per genre, their number and their average price.
  • having= : a condition on the groupshaving= keeps the groups that pass - the artists with more than one album - and a nested table follows the relation from the grouped row.
  • rollup : the total rowrollup adds the row of the grand total, with NULL in the grouped column.
  • distinctdistinct leaves out the rows that are the same as one before - every country once.

Paging

The SQL limit and the pager

  • rows= and page=rows= and page= are the SQL limit: page 2 of five is the sixth to the tenth track, read from the database and nothing more.
  • The pager{pad:pager} after the paged table counts its rows with one more statement - only when it is there - and links the pages.
  • Pages of distinct rowsDistinct rows of a join are rows of their own - thirteen albums are twelve pairs of an artist and a genre - and the pager counts those, in SQL: a count over the distinct select.

Unions

Rows of several tables in one list

  • union= : rows from two tablesThe albums and the singles in one list: a union member is a declared table, built from its declaration alone.
  • A union of severalunion= takes a list of tables; the order of the tag sorts the whole union.
  • A member with rows of its ownA union member declared with rows= - and the order that picks them - adds only those: the albums since 2016 and the two newest singles.

Output

Other shapes of the answer

  • type='record' and type='field'type= is the shape of the answer: every row, one row, or one value.
  • htmlAttrJson: JSON for an attributeThe rows as JSON, escaped for an HTML attribute - a table handed straight to a component in the browser.
  • Handling options after the SQLThe rows a select answers go through the handling options of every tag: sort=, first=, the properties of the loop.
