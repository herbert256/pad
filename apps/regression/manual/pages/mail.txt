<p>A mail is a PAD template, rendered to an HTML part and a text part and handed to a
transport. Templates live in <code>_mail/</code>, looked up like <code>_include/</code>:
<code>_mail/welcome.pad</code> is the HTML part, <code>_mail/welcome.txt</code> the text part
- without one, the text is made from the HTML - and <code>_mail/welcome.php</code> runs first.
<code>_inits.pad</code> and <code>_exits.pad</code> in the same <code>_mail/</code> are the
email's layout, with <code>@page@</code> where the mail goes:</p>

<ul>
  <li><code>from</code>, <code>cc</code>, <code>bcc</code> and <code>replyTo</code> set the other
      headers; an address is <code>ann@example.com</code> or <code>Ann &lt;ann@example.com&gt;</code>,
      several separated by commas. A line break in any of them is refused.</li>
  <li><code>$padMailTransport</code> says where it goes. <code>'file'</code>, the default, writes
      every message as an <code>.eml</code> under <code>DATA/mail/</code> instead of sending it,
      keeping the newest <code>$padMailKeep</code>; <code>'mail'</code> sends through PHP's
      <code>mail()</code>; the name of a function of the application gets the message - the
      place for SMTP through a library.</li>
  <li><code>$padMailFrom</code> is the sender; <code>$padMailLast</code> the last message of
      the request.</li>
</ul>
