/^\<h1\>CSRF protection\<\/h1\>

\<p\>Another site can make a visitor's browser post to your application \- a hidden form that
submits itself \- and the browser sends your session cookie along\. A CSRF token tells those
posts apart from the ones your own forms send\: a random value kept in the visitor's session
and written into your form, which another site can make the browser send but cannot read\.\<\/p\>

\<h2\>One setting\<\/h2\>

\<p\>With \<code\>\$padCsrf \= TRUE\<\/code\> in \<code\>_config\/config\.php\<\/code\> every
\<code\>&lt;form method\="post"&gt;\<\/code\> of a page gets a hidden \<code\>padCsrfToken\<\/code\> field
holding the token, and every POST \- and PUT, PATCH and DELETE \- that does not bring the token
back is answered \<code\>403\<\/code\> before any of the application runs\. The forms an
application already has are protected without a change to them\. A form that posts to another
site gets no token, and neither does a GET form, whose fields end up in the url\.\<\/p\>

\<h2\>The \{csrf\} tag\<\/h2\>

\<p\>\<b\>\{csrf\}\<\/b\> writes the field itself, for a form the page builds some other way, or for
an application that leaves \<code\>\$padCsrf\<\/code\> off and checks a post by hand with
\<code\>padCsrfValid \(\)\<\/code\>\. \<code\>\{csrf token\}\<\/code\> is the bare token, for a
script that sends it in an \<code\>X\-CSRF\-Token\<\/code\> header\:\<\/p\>
