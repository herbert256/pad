<p>The public holidays of twenty-eight countries, computed from rules - no service to ask, no
table of dates to keep up to date. A country is a file of <code>pad/subs/holidays/types/</code>,
named by its two-letter ISO code: at, be, br, ca, ch, cz, de, dk, es, fi, fr, gb, hu, ie, it, jp, lu, mx, nl, no, nz, pl, pt, ro,
se, sk, us and za. <b>{holidays:auto}</b> lists the holidays of a year; four helpers answer a page's PHP;
<b>{calendar:month}</b> marks them in a month.</p>

  <li>Every row has <code>date</code>, <code>name</code> (English), <code>local</code> (the
  country's own language), <code>type</code>, <code>region</code> and
  <code>substitute</code>.</li>
  <li><code>type</code> is <code>public</code> for a day off and <code>observance</code> for a
  national day that is none: Good Friday in the Netherlands, and Liberation Day there in the
  years that do not divide by five - a day off for the civil service only in 2025, 2030 ...</li>
  <li>King's Day is the 27th of April, or the 26th when the 27th is a Sunday (2025).</li>

  work on the rows; <code>lang='fr'</code> picks the language of <code>local</code> for a
  country of more than one - Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland, Canada, Finland.</li>

<h2>A region, a table</h2>

<p>A region follows its country as ISO 3166-2 writes it: <code>de-by</code> is Bavaria, with
Epiphany, Corpus Christi and All Saints' Day added - their <code>region</code> is
<code>by</code>. Germany's sixteen states, Scotland and Northern Ireland
(<code>gb-sct</code>, <code>gb-nir</code>), Alsace-Moselle and France's overseas departments
have holidays of their own. Written as a single tag, the holidays are a table.</p>

<h2>Holidays on a weekend</h2>

<p>In the United Kingdom a bank holiday on a Saturday or Sunday gets the next weekday that is
no holiday yet: Christmas 2021 on a Saturday was made up for on Monday the 27th, Boxing Day on
the Sunday on Tuesday the 28th. In the United States a federal holiday on a Saturday is
observed on the Friday before, on a Sunday on the Monday after - New Year's Day 2022 on Friday
31 December 2021. Such a day is a row of its own with <code>substitute</code> TRUE.
<code>from</code> and <code>to</code> ask for the days between two dates instead of a
year.</p>

<h2>Other calendars, other rules</h2>

<p>Romania counts Good Friday, Easter and Whitsun from the Orthodox Easter, the Julian
computus's Sunday as a Gregorian date - in 2027 five weeks after the Western one. Japan makes
up for a holiday on a Sunday on the next day that is no holiday yet, and holds a day between
two holidays as the Citizens' Holiday. New Zealand observes Anzac Day on a weekend on the
following Monday, a holiday already or not: in 2038 it is Easter Sunday, and Easter Monday is
its day. South Africa makes up for a Sunday on the Monday after it.</p>

  <li>An observance counts as a workday; a holiday on a weekend counts once - the weekend
  already is no workday - and its substitute day is a day off.</li>
  <li>A country without a file, a region it does not have or a year before 1583 is an error
  that names what there is.</li>
  <li>So is a year the country's file does not know: New Zealand's Matariki dates are fixed by
  law to 2052, and its file knows 2010 to 2052.</li>
</ul>

<h2>In the calendar</h2>

<p><code>holidays=</code> on <b>{calendar:month}</b> marks the days of a country: the cell gets the
class <code>holiday</code> - <code>observance</code> for a day that is no day off - and the
name stands under the number, in the country's language when the request's locale is in
it.</p>
