<p><code>{search 'blog', q=$q}...{/search}</code> hands over the rows of the source that the
query finds, the best first: each the source's own row with <code>$score</code> and
<code>$excerpt</code> added - the words around the first match, every matched word in a
<code>&lt;mark&gt;</code>, escaped as <code>{excerpt}</code> escapes it. It is HTML: print it
with <code>{!excerpt}</code>. Nothing found is the <code>@else@</code>; the handling options
work on the rows, <code>first=10</code> for the ten best.</p>

<p>No search server: the first search makes an inverted index of the source and keeps it in
<code>DATA/cache/search/&lt;app&gt;/</code>, made again when a file of the collection or the
rows changed. Words are reduced to their stems by the language's Snowball stemmer - the files
of <code>pad/subs/search/types/</code>: <code>en</code>, <code>nl</code>, <code>de</code>,
<code>fr</code>, <code>es</code>, <code>it</code>,
<code>pt</code>, <code>sv</code>, <code>da</code>, <code>no</code>, <code>ru</code> - and compared without their accents; stop words are
skipped. The ranking is BM25, a field weighed by its boost in <code>fields</code>.</p>

<p>A query finds a row with any of its words; a <code>"phrase"</code> must be there in that
order, <code>prefix*</code> stands for every word that begins so, <code>-word</code> leaves a
row out. Written as a single tag the hits are a numbered list. <code>padSearch</code> gives the
same rows to a page's PHP. A source that is not there, a language without a file, a
<code>fields</code> that is no list of fields or a <code>words</code> that is no number is a
strict error.</p>
