batlow

Fabio Crameri's sequential scale batlow, from a dark navy through teal, a moss green, olive and ochre to salmon and a pale pink: perceptually uniform, its lightness rising all the way, so it prints as a ramp in grey, and read by colour-vision-deficient and colour-blind eyes, as Crameri's scientific colour maps all are. The 256 colours of batlow.txt in his Scientific colour maps 8.0.1 (Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.8409685), each channel of 0 to 1 times 255, rounded. Copyright (c) 2020 Fabio Crameri, MIT licence.

Glasbey

Thirty-two colours to tell many categories apart, each chosen as far as it can be from all before it, by the method of Chris Glasbey, Gerie van der Heijden, Vivian Toh and others ("Colour displays for categorical images", Color Research and Application 32.4, 2007): started from white and black, so none is near either, and no grey among them, every colour of a chroma of 20 or more. Past eight the colours are for a map or a legend more than a chart, which has eight slots, and colour-blind eyes will confuse some of them - for them okabe-ito or a Tol palette. The first 32 of the 256 colours of colorcet's glasbey (glasbey_bw_minc_20, colorcet/__init__.py, HoloViz), each channel k/255, unchanged but for the cut; colorcet, CC BY 4.0 licence.

Turbo

Google's rainbow made smooth, by Anton Mikhailov (2019): from a dark indigo through blue, cyan, green, yellow and orange to a dark red, every step a visible change of hue, for a depth map or a field where the eye must read a value at a glance. Its lightness rises to the middle and falls again (CIELAB L* 12 at its start, 91 in the middle, 24 at its end), so it does not print as a ramp in grey, and colour-blind eyes confuse its greens with its reds - for them viridis or batlow. The 256 colours of turbo_srgb_bytes in turbo_colormap.c, the floats of turbo_colormap.py times 255 and rounded, the same floats matplotlib ships as turbo. Copyright 2019 Google LLC, Apache-2.0 licence.

vik

Fabio Crameri's diverging scale vik, from a dark navy through blue and a pale blue to a light middle and on through tan and rust to a dark red-brown, for values on both sides of a middle: perceptually uniform, its two halves nearly alike in lightness, and read by colour-vision-deficient and colour-blind eyes, as Crameri's scientific colour maps all are. The 256 colours of vik.txt in his Scientific colour maps 8.0.1 (Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.8409685), each channel of 0 to 1 times 255, rounded. Copyright (c) 2020 Fabio Crameri, MIT licence.

Viridis

A perceptually uniform scale from dark purple through blue and green to yellow, the default of matplotlib (Stéfan van der Walt and Nathaniel Smith, 2015): even steps look even, it prints in grey as a ramp, and the common forms of colour blindness read it. From matplotlib's _cm_listed.py, the 256 colours of the table as published (each channel of 0 to 1 times 255, rounded).

YlGnBu

ColorBrewer's multi-hue sequential scale YlGnBu of nine classes, from a pale yellow through green and teal to blue and a dark navy (Cynthia Brewer, colorbrewer2.org): its hue turns as its lightness falls, so the steps of a map or a heatmap tell apart better than in one hue, and ColorBrewer marks it safe for colour-blind eyes. From colorbrewer_schemes.js of ColorBrewer 2.0 (axismaps/colorbrewer), checked against d3-scale-chromatic. Apache-2.0 licence: this product includes color specifications and designs developed by Cynthia Brewer (http://colorbrewer.org/).
