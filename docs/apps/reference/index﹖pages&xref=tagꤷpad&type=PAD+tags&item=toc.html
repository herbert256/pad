<?php

  // A heading left unclosed - <h3>Orders<h3> - on a page of more than a megabyte costs that
  // heading, not the page: its scan ran on to the end of the page, gave up past PCRE's
  // limit, and the page came out empty or the request ended.

  $rows = str_repeat ( '<p>lorem ipsum</p>', 60000 );

?>

App: showcase - Page: toc/article
PHP
 HTML
toc/article.pad 		Result 
{pad:meta title='A long article', about='The contents stand above the headings it lists: it is filled in once the page has rendered, and every heading gets an id to link to.'/}
<article style="max-width:38em;line-height:1.55">
  {pad:toc title='In this article'/}

  <h2>Choosing the beans</h2>
  <p>Fresh beans matter more than any machine. Buy whole beans roasted in the last few weeks,
  and only as much as you drink in a fortnight.</p>
  <h3>Light or dark roast</h3>
  <p>A light roast keeps the fruit and acidity of its origin; a dark roast trades them for
  body and the bitter-sweet taste of the roast itself.</p>
  <h3>Single origin or blend</h3>
  <p>A blend is made to taste the same all year. A single origin tastes of one farm and one
  harvest - and changes with it.</p>

  <h2>Grinding</h2>
  <p>Grind just before brewing. A burr grinder gives even particles; a blade grinder gives
  dust and boulders that brew at different speeds.</p>
  <h3>Grind size by method</h3>
  <p>Coarse for a French press, medium for filter, fine for espresso - finer grinds extract
  faster.</p>

  <h2>Brewing</h2>
  <p>Use water just off the boil, around 94 °C, and about 60 grams of coffee per litre.</p>
  <h3>Pour-over</h3>
  <p>Bloom the grounds with a little water for thirty seconds, then pour slowly in circles.</p>
  <h3>French press</h3>
  <p>Steep four minutes, press gently, and pour it all out at once so it does not go bitter.</p>

  <h2>Storing what is left</h2>
  <p>An airtight tin in a dark cupboard. Not the fridge: the beans take on its smells.</p>
</article>
