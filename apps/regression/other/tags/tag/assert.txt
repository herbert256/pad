<p><code>{assert $total eq 42, 'the cart total'}</code> states something that must be true at
that point of the page. It always renders nothing. What it does depends on
<code>$padAssert</code>: off - the default, and so in production - the tag is skipped
entirely, the condition is not even evaluated, so an assert on a field that is not there
costs nothing and raises nothing. On, a condition that holds passes quietly and a false one is
a PAD error: <code>assert failed: $total eq 42 - the cart total</code>.</p>

<p><code>pad test &lt;app&gt;</code> switches <code>$padAssert</code> on for its run. An
application's tests live in <code>_tests/</code>: <code>cart.pad</code> (with
<code>cart.php</code> for its data) is the test page and <code>cart.txt</code> its expected
answer; a failing assert fails the test, with the condition, the message and the template
position as its report. So the checks can stand in the real pages and templates, and the
tests exercise them.</p>

<p>The parameters are read as written, not evaluated in advance - the condition is an
expression with its own operators, evaluated only after the switch has been asked. With
asserts on, an <code>{assert}</code> without a condition is an error of its own. To raise
an error unconditionally, use <a href="?tag/error">{error}</a>.</p>
